## What's real vs. what needs your hands

Being straight about this up front, because a fake lead wastes more time than an honest gap:

**Verified with live data (you can click these right now):**
- The leads in [04-fresh-hiring-posts.md](04-fresh-hiring-posts.md), pulled from HN's public API today, real permalinks. Started at 5, one (Flywheel Motion) got pulled and blocklisted after a direct report, see [07](07-companies-to-avoid.md).
- The OSS projects in [03-opensource-targets.md](03-opensource-targets.md), star counts, open-issue counts, and help-wanted labels checked live via GitHub's API today.
- The community links in [01-founder-communities.md](01-founder-communities.md), **corrected 2026-08-18** after the first pass trusted search snippets instead of checking pages directly, which let a dead-since-2015 Slack community and a dead Discord invite through. Every row now verified against the platform's own API/page directly (Discord invite-lookup API, Telegram preview pages, direct page fetches). See that file for the method per row.
- The leads `remotive_scan.py` pulls from Remotive's public API, real, dated, contract/freelance-only. Honest limit: the free API only exposes a small rolling window (currently ~17 total jobs across every category), so it's modest volume, not a firehose, but genuine and it refreshes daily.
- The leads `himalayas_scan.py` pulls from Himalayas' public API, real, and there's a lot of it (~99k jobs total). Honest limit, tested directly: the free API ignores every filter param tried (search, category, employment type), so there's no way to ask it for just "contract" or just "EMEA," it's paginated client-side filtering of the raw firehose instead, ~500 pages/~10k jobs sampled by default, `--pages`/`--all` to go deeper, with a real progress bar. Includes full-time roles too, not just contract, since a full-time EMEA/APAC role is still worth a shot.
- The leads `djinni_scan.py` pulls from Djinni's public RSS feed, real, narrowed to the categories that actually map to the stack (Python, DevOps, Golang, Data Engineer, Sysadmin, Node.js), not the general firehose (that used to let Marketing/QA/Business-Analyst postings through on a loose keyword match, fixed). Honest limit: individual listings don't include a company-name field, a lot of Djinni postings are recruiting-agency-posted without naming the client, so you'll click through for that. **Djinni is, by volume, mainly a Ukrainian job board**, confirmed directly by checking every stack-matching candidate's own page: of 282 checked in one run, 191 (68%) required either speaking Ukrainian or living in Ukraine specifically, only 91 (32%) were genuinely open, English-only, no-residency-requirement roles. The scan auto-filters the 68%, see below, so what lands in your report is the real 32%, not a source worth dropping outright since that's still a meaningful, refreshing pool of leads, but worth knowing what's under the hood.
- The leads `company_scan.py` pulls straight from 21 companies' own public job-board APIs (real, no auth), each one checked by hand before being added, not guessed from the company name: a plausible-looking Greenhouse slug can silently belong to an unrelated company reusing the same word (`wise` turned out to be a Florida sales-staffing outfit, not the fintech; `remote` a bootcamp, not Remote.com, both dropped). Region is a real filter, not a sort order: a listing only survives if its actual location is Europe (incl. UK/Nordics minus Denmark), Armenia, Georgia, or Cyprus. Trustpilot was dropped entirely (Danish-founded company, per standing preference), not just its Copenhagen postings. One company's board failing doesn't kill the run, by request, each is fetched independently and a failure just gets logged and skipped.
  **Two rounds of real false positives found and fixed by checking actual postings, not just trusting the filters:** (1) a Cloudflare listing whose list-level location just said "Hybrid" turned out, on its own page, to be Austin/NYC/SF only, so a bare "Remote"/"Hybrid" with no place name attached no longer passes; (2) **you checked N26 directly and found postings that were either JVM/Spring-Boot-only despite matching on the generic title word "backend" (no Python/TypeScript/Go anywhere in the actual requirements), or required physical presence in a specific city despite N26 offering visa/relocation support** (that's relocation, not remote work). Root cause, checked across N26's entire 72-job board: every single listing is just a bare city name ("Berlin", "Barcelona", ...), never "Remote" anywhere, confirmed by hand to mean hybrid-from-that-office, not remote-eligible. Fixed two ways: a location now needs an explicit remote-scope word in the field itself (companies that mean it say so, e.g. Canonical "Home based - EMEA", GitLab "Remote, Poland") before it's even considered, and a title match alone no longer counts, the actual description needs to require your stack, not just Java/Kotlin/Spring Boot with AWS as an afterthought. Net effect: N26 now correctly returns zero matches (it has none that are both remote and Python/TypeScript/Go), and the honest remote-eligible pool across all 21 companies is smaller (76, down from 149) but real, concentrated in the companies that are actually remote-first (Canonical, GitLab, Monzo, Sigma Software).
- The 11 companies in [08-emea-apac-startups.md](08-emea-apac-startups.md), real funding rounds with press citations, named technical contacts with real LinkedIn/company-page links. Two research passes flagged what they couldn't verify (a couple of stack fits are inferred from product description or founder background, not a direct job-posting citation, marked as such) and dropped several plausible-looking companies that didn't hold up (listed in that file's methodology section).
- All six scripts, ran successfully against live APIs while building this.
- **English-only and no forced relocation, enforced in code, not just preference.** `_common.is_english_text()` checks title + full description (script ratio catches Cyrillic/Armenian outright, common-word density catches other Latin-script languages like Turkish/French/German), `_common.requires_other_language()` separately catches a job that's written in English but explicitly requires a language you don't speak ("native Armenian speaker," "fluent Russian required"), and `_common.requires_specific_location()` catches a job that's written in English, no language requirement, but still demands physical presence somewhere ("Location: Ukraine," "(Warsaw only)," "office-based role"), **added after you flagged that Djinni postings frequently require living in Ukraine specifically**, verified against real Djinni postings ("Senior Project Manager (MilTech, Ukraine only)" correctly caught, "Location: Remote (UK or Europe)" correctly left alone) and cross-checked against ~140 real Himalayas listings for false positives before rolling it out there too (0 false positives, 2 correct catches the structured location field alone had missed, including a "USA or Canada"-restricted role). Applied to remotive_scan.py, himalayas_scan.py, and djinni_scan.py, hn_scan.py doesn't need it (Hacker News is English by nature and its own hard-skip logic already covers this).
- **Djinni's language/location requirements read from the listing's own page, not just the feed.** You flagged that some skipped Djinni jobs required living in Ukraine or speaking Ukrainian even though the RSS description never said so, checked directly: that's true, the RSS feed simply doesn't carry it. Djinni states it as structured page data instead, a "Required languages" section (language + CEFR level, e.g. "Ukrainian, C2 - Proficient") and a Schema.org JobPosting JSON-LD block (`jobLocationType`, `jobLocation`, `applicantLocationRequirements`) that name the eligible country or countries directly, both invisible to the feed text. `djinni_scan.py` now fetches each stack-matching candidate's own page (after the free RSS-level checks already ran) and reads both, so a job requiring Ukrainian or restricted to Ukraine/Poland/wherever gets auto-skipped with the real reason instead of needing you to catch and tag it by hand. If that one extra fetch fails for a given listing (deleted job, network hiccup), it's kept rather than silently dropped, a warning prints so you can eyeball it.

**Not possible for me to do, and why:**
- **Scrape Slack/Discord/Telegram for "one recent gig posted in each community."** These platforms render via JavaScript and require a logged-in session; there's no public API for browsing a channel's history without membership. I could not fabricate a plausible-sounding fake post and hand it to you as real, that would be worse than saying nothing. Instead: real join links plus exactly which channel to check once you're in.
- **LinkedIn's "recently posted" feed, X-ray your network, or run searches as you.** LinkedIn requires an authenticated session and its ToS prohibits automated scraping; I have no login. What I *can* do, and did, is write out the actual boolean syntax and filter combinations that work, so you run them yourself in under 5 minutes.
- **Reddit.** Reddit blocks non-browser traffic outright (I hit a hard 403 testing this, both via direct fetch and API). Real subreddit names and saved-search URLs are in the docs; the live post-pulling has to happen in your own logged-in browser.
- **Wellfound founder contact info.** Wellfound intentionally gates direct contact behind its own messaging/apply system, that's the product, not an oversight, and scraping around it would cross from "research" into circumventing a platform's access controls. I found real, recently-funded companies instead; you reach founders through the normal channel (Wellfound message, or their public Twitter/LinkedIn/company email if listed).
- **15 verified fresh "need help" posts.** I got 5 solid, real, dated ones from HN (the only source with a public, scriptable API). The other 10 you'd get from Reddit/Twitter/LinkedIn/Discord live search, all blocked for the reasons above. The `hn_scan.py` script keeps this list refreshing itself, which is the actual fix for "this goes stale in a week."

## Fastest path to a first message sent today

2. Run `make scan` (hn + oss + remotive + himalayas + djinni + companies back to back, himalayas and companies each take a few minutes and show a progress bar, the rest are quick), writes today's dated reports into `scan-results/<today>/` (or does nothing to a source if it's already there), open them in your editor. Clickable links, a checkbox per lead. After you apply somewhere, tick its box (`- [ ]` to `- [x]`) and save, that file won't be touched again until a new day's scan creates the next one.

## On positioning

Every template in here is written the same way: senior generalist (AWS/cloud architecture, Python, TypeScript, Go, Linux, 20 years combined between you and Yanovian LLC), now based in Armenia, open to competitive rates and fast starts. That's a genuine advantage (strong senior background plus cost/timezone flexibility), not a discount pitch, the copy treats it that way. Nothing here references your job-search situation, urgency, or that these materials exist; that's between you and this repo.

**Location and legal status, for accuracy when tailoring pitches:** based in Armenia, with Estonian e-Residency (a digital business ID for invoicing through an EU entity, not work authorization) and a Danish residence permit (from the years there, tied to Denmark specifically, not full EU freedom of movement). None of that grants US work authorization, so any role that explicitly requires it (like the doubling.io lead in [04](04-fresh-hiring-posts.md)) is a genuine skip, not just a caveat. It doesn't affect standard contract/B2B engagements, since those pay a foreign entity for services rather than employ a person. Timezone is flexible, but **EMEA and APAC companies are the actual preference**, US ones tend to be stricter about contracting with an Armenia-based entity (banking/compliance friction on top of the work-authorization question), so the scan scripts and newer docs lean that direction: `remotive_scan.py` auto-skips US-only listings and sorts EMEA/APAC/Worldwide ones first, and [08-emea-apac-startups.md](08-emea-apac-startups.md) targets that region specifically rather than the US-heavy [05-wellfound-startups.md](05-wellfound-startups.md).

The message templates are written to sound like one founder talking to another, not a marketing pitch: short, plain, contractions, no corporate polish. If you tweak them, keep that bar, a message that reads like it was typed fast and means it beats one that reads like it was drafted.
